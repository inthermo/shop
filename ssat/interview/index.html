The facts

Is it required?Hotchkiss lists "Schedule an Interview" as step 2 of applying, and its FAQ calls it recommended (required only for homeschooled applicants). Treat it as expected and book it.
WhenInterviews run mid-September to mid-January. None over Thanksgiving or Winter Break. The FAQ warns the calendar "fills up quickly, especially Saturday appointments." Book early.
Before you can bookSubmit the Admission Inquiry Form on hotchkiss.org.
FormatOn campus, which is what Hotchkiss "strongly encourage[s]." The visit is reported to start with a student-led tour, then a one-on-one with an admission counselor. The tour guide is a current student: ask them real questions too, since it's the most candid view of the school you'll get.
Length & shapePlan on about 30–45 minutes for the interview itself (that's the published length for the virtual version; the office can confirm the on-campus schedule). Student first, one-on-one; parents are usually brought in briefly at the end. Families can't sit in on classes or stay overnight during the visit.
ToneThe FAQ says it is "intended to be a dialogue rather than an evaluative experience" about "your interests, your talents, and your hopes for the future."
DeadlinesApplication and financial aid: Jan 15, 2027. Decisions: Mar 10, 2027. Reply by: Apr 10, 2027. Fee $75. No separate interview-by date is published; applying after Jan 15 means no on-campus interview that winter.
TestsSSAT or ISEE for grades 9–10. Application goes through Gateway to Prep Schools.
Admission office(860) 435-3102 · admission@hotchkiss.org · 11 Interlaken Rd, Lakeville, CT 06039. Director of Admission and Financial Aid: Ted Weihman.

When booking, ask the office how long to allow for tour plus interview and whether parents meet the counselor separately. Two Hotchkiss pages disagree on "required" vs. "recommended"; booking makes the question moot.

What Hotchkiss says it looks for

  • Students who "show extraordinary character, independence, and good citizenship." Every part of the application is weighed equally.
  • Admitted students typically have strong grades, "excellent teacher recommendations," and "strong writing skills."
  • Mission: Hotchkiss "seeks to inspire a diverse range of students who are committed to the betterment of self and society," cultivating imagination and intellect, openness and personal integrity, and empathy and responsible citizenship. Students should be "fully engaged in the world."
  • Community values it names: high standards of scholarship and conduct; respect, honesty and compassion; taking risks and reflecting on them; service and environmental stewardship.

Translation for the interview: they want to hear curiosity, initiative, how you treat people, and what you do when something is hard. Not a list of awards.

How to prepare Hotchkiss's own 8 steps

  1. Research the school. Bring up one specific thing that drew you (a program, Fairfield Farm, a club, a class). See Why Hotchkiss.
  2. Be early. Arrive 10–15 minutes ahead. Lakeville is rural; check the drive time and build in slack.
  3. Dress neatly and sit up. First impressions are made before the first question.
  4. Be authentic. In their words: "If you only give the answers you think your interviewers want to hear, your words will ring false."
  5. Talk about yourself without boasting. Tell a short story that shows the quality instead of naming it.
  6. Practice the sample questions out loud, with a parent or in the mock interview below. Don't memorize scripts; know your three or four stories.
  7. It's a two-way street. Come with real questions. See Questions to ask.
  8. Send a thank-you note within 24 hours. Template below.

From other admission guides: the student speaks for herself; parents support but don't take over. Expect to be asked about any rough spots on the transcript, so have a calm, honest answer ready.

Questions to ask the interviewer

Pick two or three. The first seven are suggested by Hotchkiss. Skip anything the website already answers.

  • What is a typical day like for a ninth grader?
  • What are the most popular sports teams and clubs?
  • What are the opportunities for student leadership?
  • How does course selection work for new students?
  • What activities are offered on weekends?
  • What is the orientation program like?
  • What kind of student thrives here?
  • How does the school support new boarders in the first few months?
  • How do students balance academics, sports, and arts?
  • What do you (the interviewer) like most about working here?

Why Hotchkiss: facts to draw on

Founded1891, Lakeville, Connecticut (northwest corner of the state).
SizeAbout 600 students, grades 9–12 plus postgraduates. Over 150 faculty, 72% with advanced degrees. Site lists a 4:1 student-to-faculty ratio.
Fairfield FarmA 287-acre school farm a mile from campus where students plant and harvest organic produce and care for farm animals. Easy, specific thing to mention if sustainability or hands-on work interests you.
Programs on the siteHumanities Program, the Class of 2017 EFX Lab (engineering/fabrication). Look at the courses and clubs pages before the interview and pick one or two that match your real interests.
Mission words"Betterment of self and society" · imagination and intellect · openness and integrity · empathy and responsible citizenship · service and environmental stewardship.
Head of SchoolDr. Anne Bruder (site references her investiture; confirm she is current before name-dropping).

Your "why Hotchkiss" should connect you to them: one thing you already do or love, plus the specific Hotchkiss offering where you would keep doing it. Generic praise ("great academics") says nothing.
